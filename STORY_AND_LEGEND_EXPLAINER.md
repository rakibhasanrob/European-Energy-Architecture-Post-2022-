# The Redrawing of European Energy: A Tale of Two Maps

## Overview: The Great Realignment
For half a century, European industrial power was built on a fundamental geographic premise: cheap, reliable energy piped westward from Russia across fixed steel corridors. In 2022, following the invasion of Ukraine and the subsequent weaponization and sabotage of Baltic gas infrastructure, that foundational architecture collapsed almost overnight.

These two maps capture the before-and-after of the largest, fastest infrastructural realignment in modern history.

---

## Part 1: Pre-2022 Architecture (Decades of Pipeline Dependency)

### The Narrative
Before 2022, Europe functioned as Russia's primary energy customer, taking over 155 billion cubic meters of natural gas each year (roughly 40 percent of the European Union's supply) along with 2.2 million barrels of crude oil every day.

This relationship was cemented by thousands of kilometers of buried steel:
- In the north, the subsea Nord Stream twin corridors connected Siberian gas directly to Lubmin in northeastern Germany, bypassing all transit nations.
- Through the central European plain, the Yamal-Europe pipeline carried Siberian gas across Belarus and Poland into Germany.
- Through the south, the Soviet-era Brotherhood network channeled gas across Ukraine into Austria's central trading hub at Baumgarten.
- Alongside natural gas ran the massive Druzhba ("Friendship") oil pipeline, which originated in Russia's Volga-Urals basin, split at the Mozyr junction in Belarus, and directly supplied German industrial refineries in Schwedt and Leuna, as well as plants in Czechia, Slovakia, and Hungary.
- Out of the Black Sea, Russia's deep-water port at Novorossiysk loaded supertankers destined for Italian refineries via the Bosporus.

For decades, European industry operated under the belief that economic interdependence guaranteed mutual stability. Fixed pipelines created fixed political commitments.

### How to Read the Pre-2022 Legend
- Solid Blue Lines (Russian Gas Pipelines): High-capacity pipelines delivering natural gas directly from Siberian extraction basins to German, Austrian, and Central European hubs.
- Solid Orange Line (Druzhba Crude Oil Pipeline): The overland crude pipeline network feeding refineries in Eastern Germany and Central Europe.
- Dashed Teal Lines (Maritime Crude Tankers): Seaborne oil routes ferrying crude out of Russia's Black Sea port of Novorossiysk to Mediterranean terminals like Trieste.
- Thin Faint Lines (Baseline Supply): Pre-existing non-Russian baseline gas imports from Norway and North Africa.
- Shaded Country Polygons:
  - Red Fill (Russia): The primary supplying basin.
  - Amber Fill (Belarus and Ukraine): The overland transit corridor countries.
  - Slate-Indigo Fill (EU Nations): The consumer off-takers and industrial end-users.

## Part 2: Post-2022 Pivot (Severed Corridors and the Global LNG Bridge)

### The Narrative
The geopolitical rupture of 2022 shattered this architecture in less than eighteen months.

As political tensions peaked, pipeline flows were progressively curtailed. In September 2022, underwater detonations crippled three of the four Nord Stream pipes in the Baltic Sea, permanently ending direct Baltic gas deliveries. Poland and Germany nationalized transit assets, reversing and shutting down the Yamal-Europe and Druzhba North pipelines. Overland gas transit through Ukraine was reduced to a fraction of its former volume.

Deprived of its primary supply, Europe executed an emergency logistical pivot:
1. The Transatlantic LNG Bridge: Nations without regasification capacity rapidly contracted Floating Storage Regasification Units (FSRUs)—specialized vessels permanently moored at ports such as Wilhelmshaven, Brunsbüttel, Świnoujście, Klaipėda, and Alexandroupolis. An armada of liquefied natural gas tankers from the United States and Qatar surged across the Atlantic to offload supercooled fuel directly into the European grid.
2. The Norwegian Backbone: Norway expanded extraction and inaugurated the 10-billion-cubic-meter Baltic Pipe through Denmark into Poland, officially displacing Russia as Europe's largest piped natural gas supplier.
3. The Southern Corridors: Gas from Azerbaijan's Shah Deniz field in the Caspian Sea began flowing at maximum throughput via the Trans-Adriatic Pipeline (TAP) into southern Italy, while Algeria maximized deliveries across the Mediterranean through the TransMed pipeline.

By 2024, Russian piped gas accounted for less than 10 percent of European imports. Europe had traded fixed overland reliance on a single neighbor for flexible, maritime access to the global seaborne market.

### How to Read the Post-2022 Legend
- Dashed Red Lines with X-Markers (Severed / Halted Russian Lines): Sabotaged or shut-down corridors, prominently highlighting the Nord Stream rupture in the Baltic Sea, the terminated Yamal-Europe line, and the embargoed Druzhba North branch.
- Vibrant Green Lines (Alternative Pipelines): New and maximized non-Russian pipeline arteries, including Norway's Baltic Pipe, the Trans-Adriatic Pipeline from Azerbaijan, and TransMed from Algeria.
- Dashed Cyan Arcs (Transatlantic LNG Carrier Arteries): Global maritime shipping lanes bringing LNG cargoes from the Atlantic and Mediterranean into coastal European terminals.
- Dotted Muted Line (Restricted Trickle Flows): The residual, precarious gas volumes still moving through the Ukrainian corridor.
- Shaded Country Polygons:
  - Red Fill (Russia): The sanctioned exporter, largely severed from the European pipeline network.
  - Amber Fill (Belarus and Ukraine): Severed and restricted transit territory.
  - Green Fill (Norway, Azerbaijan, Algeria): Alternative sovereign pipeline suppliers stepping in to fill the supply gap.
  - Slate-Indigo Fill (EU Nations): Diversified consumer nations sourcing energy from global maritime markets and regional partners.
- Node Badges:
  - Red Hex Badges: Rupture and severance zones (e.g., Nord Stream Rupture, Schwedt PCK supply disruption).
  - Cyan Anchor / Terminal Medallions: Critical LNG import gateways and newly commissioned FSRU berths (Wilhelmshaven, Gate Rotterdam, Dunkirk, Świnoujście, Klaipėda, Alexandroupolis).

## Key Takeaway
Pipelines are geography turned into destiny; once built, they lock buyers and sellers into mutual dependence. The transformation of European energy architecture between 2022 and 2024 demonstrates how quickly modern infrastructure can adapt when geopolitical necessity forces a transition from fixed continental geography to global maritime supply lines.
